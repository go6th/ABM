# Signals reference

The full specification is the user's Account Intent Signals document. This file is the working summary.

## Tiers

**Tier 1, budget proximate.** New leader in an owning role. Job postings for work the client delivers. A named platform initiative or funded program. A forced platform move such as an ERP support deadline. Incumbent partner change or a stalled rollout. An RFP or vendor evaluation. Category research on review sites.

**Tier 2, one step removed.** Mergers and acquisitions, checked for whether they closed. New product launches that strain commercial and service systems. Technology stack changes with first seen dates. Reorganizations. Cost and efficiency programs. Regulatory and quality events. Financial position against peers. How the company describes itself in filings. Activist investors. New capability centres or shared service hubs. Events sponsored or exhibited at.

**Tier 3, context and openers.** Executive posts. Speaking past and upcoming. CEO interviews and analyst coverage. Employee content. Unintended breadcrumbs. Vendor engagement on LinkedIn. Review sites and communities. Earnings themes. Competitor moves and peer KPIs that can be offered as a give. Customer and partner name matching against client references.

## Shelf life

Tier 1 signals are good for about 90 days. Leadership changes are strongest in the first six months. Technology detections hold while last seen stays current. Anything older than 18 months moves to a context and history table, where it can explain direction but is not a reason to reach out.

## Recording rules

Each signal row carries the finding, grade, tier, lanes, the date it occurred or was posted, and source IDs. Fit is scored once and is never adjusted by signals. None is a valid result for a row.

## Lane fingerprint questions

For each lane, try to answer these from evidence and turn the gaps into first call questions.

- **Salesforce and service:** which clouds, which add ons (CPQ, Marketing Cloud, Field Service, MuleSoft for integration), who owns the platform in IT, who owns it in the business, where the platform team sits, which partner is incumbent, what is being hired for.
- **ServiceNow:** which modules, who owns the platform, who the incumbent partner is, whether the company presents at Knowledge.
- **SAP:** ECC or S/4HANA and how far along, which satellite products (IBP, MDG, Ariba, Datasphere, Analytics Cloud, EWM, Signavio), who runs transformation, whether it is staffed in house.
- **Tableau and analytics:** which BI tools run side by side, where Tableau builders sit, whether it is Server or Cloud, who owns analytics for commercial and service functions, whether a Microsoft first direction is visible.
- **Veeva:** which Veeva products are in use (Veeva CRM on the Salesforce platform versus Vault CRM, Vault PromoMats, MedComms, Vault Clinical, RIM, Quality, Safety), whether a Veeva CRM to Vault CRM migration is planned or underway and its target date, how Veeva connects to Salesforce and to the data platform, who owns commercial systems and who owns Vault in R&D and Quality, which partner is incumbent, what is being hired for, whether the company presents at Veeva Summit.
- **Data and AI:** warehouse and lake platforms, integration tools, AI frameworks and hyperscalers named in postings, whether builds are in house, named data programs, the contact centre stack.

## Patterns worth looking for

- Hidden titles. Platform roles are often titled generically, such as a DevSecOps or product development title for a senior Salesforce developer. Keyword search on the description finds these; title search does not.
- Seams between systems. Postings that name SAP, Salesforce and a field service tool together point to integration pain.
- A capability centre expansion plus platform team titles in that city shows where the work is moving.
- A title containing the word migration, transformation or program names funded work.
- Several BI tools named in one posting signals analytics sprawl.
