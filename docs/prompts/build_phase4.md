Build Phase 4 ("Make the system work between runs: refresh on a schedule, watch accounts for signals, and tell sellers what changed"). It is one prompt with **ten ordered workstreams** (section 4); finish and gate each before the next. It builds on the earlier packages (workflow, ledger and credit cap, parallel execution, routing and verifier, quality gate, exports, review and resume, observability, Phase 3 tenant app) and reuses them; it does not rebuild them. The two ways this phase can fail are **alert fatigue** and **monitoring cost growing with accounts times signals**, plus **false positives from keyword matches**; every design choice below serves those three. Real pilots, real monitors, real CRM sandboxes, real cloud restores and the 20-account and 70% targets cannot be done here; they ship as measured-ready tooling and are reported **Blocked**, never as met.

## 2. Anti-hallucination protocol (build time)

1. **No invented numbers.** The sources give cadences (above) and the half-lives hiring 21, technology 90, leadership 180, funding 365 days (those are **confidence decay**, not shelf life). They give **no**: shelf-life durations, the delta-score formula or threshold, the maximum brief age, significance weights or thresholds, per-user rate caps, digest size thresholds, the per-account news cap value, the monitoring budget amount or throttle ladder, retry limits for a CRM, load targets, or on-call details. Each is configuration with `source: TODO, needs product owner` or `status: proposal`, set conservatively (fewer alerts, lower spend). ARCH cost figures are illustrative; the system reports **measured** ratios only.
2. **Proposals of mine, not facts from the sources** (ADR and register entries at the next free numbers; earlier prompts used D-12 to D-70, so start at **D-71**; new conflicts at the next free `C` number):
   - **D-71 shelf life:** a `shelf_life` table per signal type (job postings, leadership, news, technology detections, executive voice, and the rest of the checklist rows) separate from the grading half-lives; cadence from SaaS 5.1; rows whose cadence is `null` are **not scheduled** until an analyst sets one; every value carries `source` or `status`; versioned and tenant-overridable only within bounds.
   - **D-72 probe and escalation:** delta score as a documented pure function over typed delta items, threshold and maximum brief age as config, a minimum interval between escalations, a per-account escalation cap per period; an escalation reuses the probe's pulls (inherited read-only ledger) so nothing is pulled twice.
   - **D-73 deduplication and significance:** deterministic event fingerprints (event type, normalised entity, normalised attributes, date bucket); near-duplicates from different outlets are clustered by fixed rules, **ambiguous pairs are linked and delivered in one alert listing both sources, never silently merged or dropped**; the significance score is a pure, explainable weighted function (signal type, grade, source tier, recency, lane match, priority) whose breakdown is stored with the alert.
   - **D-74 alert defaults:** the ten rules from `assets/alert_rules.yaml` plus two proposed digest rules for **facility expansion** and **vendor customer story** (the monitors exist in the roadmap, the rules do not); a new user starts with the weekly digest only and **subscribes up** to immediate rules and thresholds; per-user and per-account caps; snooze and mute per account.
   - **D-75 leads:** a keyword or listing match creates a **Lead**; a Lead alone never triggers an immediate-tier alert; immediate rules require the item to have been **read and verified in this refresh** at the grade the rule names; an unverified Lead can appear only in the digest, labelled "Lead, not yet verified".
   - **D-76 sensitive findings:** detection by event category in code (breach, layoff, lawsuit and their mapped event types), delivered from **fixed templates** labelled "Preparation only, not an opening line", never in an immediate-tier subject, never suggested as a hook; a wording lint blocks outreach-style phrasing in every channel.
   - **D-77 CRM write-back (conflict: the roadmap allows "enriched account fields" but also "new records only, never overwrite"; updating a field on an existing Account is an overwrite of an existing record):** the adapters have **no update or delete method at all**; notes and tasks are new records; enriched fields go into **new snapshot records** (a dedicated object or note the customer's admin installs), not into existing fields; writing into an empty field of an existing record needs my explicit decision (default no). Salesforce first, HubSpot if time allows. Content is **public-evidence output only** (never Internal Only, never sensitive findings, never provider names or credits); the account-to-CRM mapping is confirmed by a tenant admin and an ambiguous match writes nothing; opt-in per tenant, per-batch approval by default.
   - **D-78 Open Door:** a versioned signal event schema and a read API plus signed webhooks (same signing as alerts), additive-only inside v1, documented with schemas and examples, contract-tested; **no execution features, no consumers, no sequencing, no sending**.
   - **D-79 monitoring budget:** a tenant monthly allowance in the tenant's plan units (D-63: shown as percent, never raw credits or dollars), internal dollar caps in the operator plane, an alert at **80%** (sourced), a throttle ladder after that (probe-only on lowest-score accounts first, then lengthened cadences, then pause of non-priority monitors; priority A and the budget alert itself last), a hard stop at 100%, monthly reset without rollover; distinct from the wallet and from per-run caps.
   - **D-80 hardening scope:** what can be proven hermetically (restore into a scratch Postgres from a base backup plus WAL and re-run the invariants and the isolation suite; a load test against stub providers; a scripted failover rehearsal against local services) versus what needs the real GCP staging (Cloud SQL restore and failover, regional DR). RPO under 60 s and RTO of 30 minutes stay **targets proven by a game-day**, per the sources.
3. **Verify every library and platform fact** (broker monitor and webhook capabilities and signatures, Slack app scopes and rate limits, Salesforce and HubSpot create APIs and sandbox behaviour, Cloud Scheduler and Cloud Tasks semantics, Cloud SQL backup, PITR and failover behaviour, k6 options) against installed packages and first-party docs; mark anything unverified `[VERIFY]` in `docs/open-items.md`. ## 3. Anti-hallucination protocol (run time)

Each rule has a "lying scenario" test (section 7). Prefixes: **RF** refresh, **SL** shelf life, **SM** monitors, **DS** dedupe and scoring, **AL** alerting, **CW** CRM write-back, **OD** Open Door, **CG** cost governance, **HD** hardening.

| # | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| RF1 | **A refresh continues the account, it does not redo it.** It starts from the latest tracker version, creates a **new** tracker version, re-runs High priority rows and rows past their shelf life, and leaves the rest untouched and visibly "not due". Every earlier version, file and fact stays. |
| RF2 | **Expire, never delete.** An expired signal gets `superseded_at`, is copied into the insert-only history table with the reason and the run that expired it, and is excluded from current tables and counts; history is queryable by the app and by exports of past versions. |
| RF3 | **Source IDs are stable.** The allocator resolves a source by canonical URL and content identity; a source seen before keeps its code in every later Summary and Evidence file; a new source gets the next code; a code is never reused for a different source (property test over many refresh cycles). |
| RF4 | **Probe first, escalate by rule.** The probe touches only the configured cheap sources; escalation is a pure decision (delta score, maximum age, caps) recorded with its inputs; an escalation never repeats a pull the probe made (pull once, inherited ledger); a failed probe source makes its rows **Blocked with a reason**, not "no change". |
| RF5 | **Debounce removals.** A posting or item absent once is `missing_once`; it becomes REMOVED only after the configured two consecutive probes; a reappearance cancels the pending removal; an outage or a Blocked source never counts as absence. |
| RF6 | **The delta is computed by code and is the only input to the Summary diff and the alert engine.** New, expired, regraded and count changes with their base, reconstructible from append-only facts; an empty delta is stated ("No change", with what was checked and when), never omitted. |
| RF7 | **Diff-first Summary.** The new Summary version opens with "What changed since <version and date>" rendered from the delta by template (no model), keeps the earlier version, passes the quality gate and the Phase 3 row-parity check; sensitive items follow D-76 wording. |
| RF8 | **Exactly one probe or refresh per account slot.** Slots are keyed `(account, kind, period)` with a unique constraint; a lost scheduler tick, a duplicate delivery and an overlap with a manual run produce one execution; after an outage, missed slots are caught up in a bounded, jittered way (no stampede), oldest priority first, within tenant and provider concurrency limits. |
| SL1 | **The policy is data with provenance.** Every shelf life and cadence has a `source` or `status`; the due-date of a row is computed from `last_checked_at` plus its shelf life in a pure function; `null` cadence means "never scheduled", not "default weekly". Changing the policy never rewrites history; it affects future due dates only. |
| SM1 | **Monitors are watchers with typed outputs.** Six monitor kinds (build-role postings, leadership changes, news events, technology detection changes, facility expansions, vendor customer stories), each a registered adapter over the connector layer, using broker monitoring where the provider catalogue offers it and a scheduled poll otherwise; every call goes through `call_operation` (ledger, cap, rate limits); a monitor with no available source is `NotConfigured` and its row Blocked. |
| SM2 | **Inbound monitor webhooks are hostile.** Signature and timestamp verified with the provider's scheme, unknown source rejected, event id deduplicated, payload stored then handed to the **tool-less extractor** and the verifier; nothing from the payload is trusted as a fact, an instruction or a URL to fetch (SSRF rules apply). |
| SM3 | **A match is a Lead until read** (D-75). The grading engine decides the grade; a monitor can only propose a candidate with its evidence; an unverifiable candidate is dropped with a counted reason, never promoted. |
| SM4 | **Monitors are bounded and priced.** Per-account monitor sets come from the tenant's lanes and the plan; each monitor run reserves through the credit gate; a monitor cannot exceed its account's share of the monitoring budget (CG rules); per-monitored-account cost is recorded from the first run. |
| DS1 | **One event is one alert.** Events are fingerprinted (D-73); the dedupe key `(rule, account, fingerprint)` has a unique constraint; the same event arriving from a second source, a re-fetch, a replayed webhook or a later probe attaches as extra evidence to the existing event and **never** creates a second alert; ambiguous clusters follow D-73. |
| DS2 | **Scoring is pure and explainable.** Inputs are typed fields from graded facts; the score, its components and the config version are stored with the event; the same inputs always give the same score (property test); tie-breaks are deterministic; no model computes or adjusts a score. |
| DS3 | **Thresholds filter, they do not delete.** An event below a user's threshold is stored and can be browsed in the app and digest settings; only delivery is suppressed. |
| AL1 | **Every alert is traceable.** Each alert row has a non-null `fact_delta_id` pointing at a graded, sourced delta item; the alert text is built from that item by template; an alert with no delta cannot be created (database constraint plus test). |
| AL2 | **Rules come from one registry.** The ten rules load from `assets/alert_rules.yaml` (validated against `scripts/validate_assets.py`), plus the D-74 additions marked `status: proposal`; tenant and user rules are scoped to the tenant's own accounts by RLS and cannot name another tenant's account (test); a rule cannot reference a grade or field that the grading engine does not define. |
| AL3 | **Subscribe up.** A new user, account or tenant receives the weekly digest only; immediate rules, Slack and thresholds must be switched on by the user; defaults are config; a bulk "subscribe to all" is an explicit action and audited. |
| AL4 | **Noise control is enforced in code.** Per-account daily cap for tier-one news, per-user hourly and daily caps (proposals), debounce, dedupe, digest batching above the tenant threshold, grade floors (technology ignores below C), digest-only rules never real-time (confidence drop); a suppressed alert is recorded with its reason and counted. |
| AL5 | **Sensitive wording is fixed.** Breach, layoff and lawsuit events render only from the D-76 templates in every channel; the lint rejects phrases that suggest using the event to open a conversation; such events are never in a subject line of an immediate email and never offered as a first-call question. |
| AL6 | **Delivery is an outbox with a measured SLO.** Written in the same transaction as the alert; at-least-once with idempotent consumers keyed by event id; channels: in-app, email, Slack, signed webhook (HMAC-SHA256 over timestamp and body, 5 minute tolerance, exponential retries for 24 h); bounded retries then a recorded failure and an operator-visible state; a delivery failure never blocks a refresh. The alert-freshness SLI (95% within 24 h of the signal) is computed from `signal_detected_at` and `delivered_at` and exported to observability. |
| AL7 | **Client-safe by construction.** Alerts, digests, Slack messages, emails, webhooks and status pages pass `scripts/scan_client_output.py` with the denylist: no provider names, credit counts, cost, call references or other accounts; links are authenticated app links with no bearer power; Slack and email carry the minimum needed. |
| AL8 | **Usefulness is measured with its base.** In-app rating (useful, not useful, fixed reasons) per alert; the metric is useful / rated, reported with rated / delivered, per tenant and as an operator aggregate; small bases are labelled; the 70% target is shown as a target, never as a result. |
| CW1 | **The adapter cannot overwrite.** The CRM client interface exposes create operations and read-only lookups only; no update, upsert-over-existing, merge or delete method exists; a fake CRM server that rejects every non-create request proves no code path issues one; a mutation check removes the guard and the test fails. |
| CW2 | **Writes are idempotent and reconcilable.** Each write has a key `(tenant, account, kind, content hash)` in an insert-only `crm_writes` ledger with intent and result rows; a crash after the CRM create and before the ledger result is resolved by a read-only search for the write's marker and adopted, never repeated; a replay returns the stored result. |
| CW3 | **Opt-in, mapped, approved.** Off by default; a tenant admin enables it per CRM; each account's CRM record mapping is confirmed by a human, and an ambiguous or missing match writes nothing; a preview shows exactly what will be created; the default requires approval per batch; revoking the connection stops writes at once. |
| CW4 | **What may be written is whitelisted.** Only public-evidence content from delivered, gate-passed output (never Internal Only, sensitive findings, provider names, credit counts or cost); payloads are rendered by template and scanned before sending; field and size limits are enforced; a failed scan blocks the write. |
| CW5 | **Never outreach.** Tasks are to-dos for the owner with preparation wording; the code has no path that sends an email, message or sequence; every write is audited and visible to the tenant. |
| OD1 | **A contract, not a feature.** Signal events and the read API are defined by versioned JSON Schemas with examples and a changelog; additive changes only inside v1; contract tests validate every emitted event and every API response against the schema; breaking changes need a new version and Sunset headers; there is no consumer, scheduler or action code behind it. |
| OD2 | **Same safety as alerts.** Tenant-scoped, role-gated, RLS-enforced, cursor-paginated, audited, client-safe; webhooks signed as in AL6; payloads carry the graded fact reference, source code and grade, never raw broker payloads or provider names. |
| CG1 | **The monitoring budget is a hard, atomic limit.** Every probe and monitor run reserves against the tenant's monthly monitoring allowance by a conditional update; parallel jobs can never exceed it; unknown cost counts as the upper bound; an exhausted allowance denies with a typed state, never a silent skip. |
| CG2 | **80% alerts once; throttling is deterministic.** The 80% crossing emits exactly one event and one alert per tenant per period (admin, immediate tier); the throttle ladder (D-79) is a pure function of used share and account scores; throttled accounts show "monitoring reduced" in the app; priority A and the budget alert are last to be reduced; reset happens once at the period boundary. |
| CG3 | **Cost per monitored account is first-class.** Derived from the ledger by account, monitor kind and period (integer micro-USD, operator plane, reconciled with the ledger as in observability); the refresh-to-full-run **ratio** is computed by a report from measured rows with its base, count and as-of, and states "not enough data" instead of a number when the base is too small. |
| HD1 | **Restore is proven, not assumed.** A scripted hermetic restore (base backup plus WAL replay into a scratch Postgres) is followed by the invariants: ledger sums, one settlement per terminal run, the RLS catalog, the isolation suite, hash chains, a reconciler pass that re-sends queued runs; the drill records measured RPO and RTO of the rehearsal and says it is a rehearsal. |
| HD2 | **Load tests state their model.** A k6 and synthetic-injector profile (tenants, accounts, probes per hour, alert fan-out, API reads) runs against stub providers; results (latency percentiles, errors, queue ages, database pool and lease use, cost of telemetry) are reported with the profile; no pass or fail target is asserted that I did not give except the sourced SLOs, which are reported against. |
| HD3 | **Operations pack is complete and tested.** Runbooks (each tested by the observability runbook linter), a DR plan with the sourced seven steps adapted to GCP, an on-call rota template with placeholders, a status page generated from health data and scheduled maintenance entries (publishing is `NotConfigured` until I choose a host), and a postmortem template; no real names, numbers or channels. |

## 6. Loops (all bounded, recorded, testable)
Scheduler tick (bounded selection with `SKIP LOCKED`, per-tenant and per-provider fairness, jitter); catch-up after an outage (bounded per tick); probe and escalation (one escalation per decision, capped); monitor poll (page- and quota-bounded, resumable by cursor); webhook processing (one pass, idempotent); digest assembly (bounded, deterministic order); outbox relay (bounded retries, 24 h window for webhooks); CRM write (single attempt per key, reconcile pass, no blind retry); budget reset (once per period); restore and load drills (bounded steps).
